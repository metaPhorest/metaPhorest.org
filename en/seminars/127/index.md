#### Fieldwork and Narratives Exploring the Boundaries Between Humans and Nonhumans (Animals and Living Beings)  
In this seminar, artist Masaya Mizuta, who explores the contradictions and tensions inherent in relationships between animals and humans through local fieldwork and interviews, and Akira Fujioka, who investigates life from both humanities and scientific perspectives through silkworms and other subjects, will discuss the complex intersections between human society and nonhuman beings.
In the first half, Mizuta will trace the development of his artistic practice, from his early works as a product design student to his Grand Prix-winning work at *Kobe Rokko Meets Art 2024 beyond*, and his project *Kaiko-roku* in Toyota City. Connecting the perspective of products—objects whose functions and forms are shaped by human needs—with research into local industrial heritage and history, he will explore how humans have managed and systematized living organisms and nature, and how these processes can lead us to reconsider the relationships underlying them. In the second half, Fujioka will introduce his approaches to studying life, particularly through silkworms. His research examines the nature of silkworms, which cannot survive without human care, as well as the material and environmental dimensions of life, drawing on both historical contexts and scientific observations.
The session will explore how, in attempting to control living organisms and objects, humans themselves gradually become incorporated into the very systems they create. Moving beyond a human-centered perspective, we will consider what surrounds us and how we might coexist with beings different from ourselves.  
  
#### Masaya Mizuta  
Born in Aichi Prefecture in 1998 and currently based in Kyoto, Masaya Mizuta graduated from the Design Strategy Course at the Graduate School of Design, Kyushu University. His artistic practice explores the relationships between animals and humans. Through research primarily based on interviews about how humans interact with particular animals, he identifies contradictions and moments of discomfort that become starting points for his artistic works. He mainly works with video, mechanical devices, text, and sound. His recent exhibitions and presentations include *Koganecho Bazaar 2026*, *Kobe Rokko Meets Art 2024 beyond*, and *ATAMI ART GRANT 2023*. His major awards include the Grand Prix at *WACCA ART AWARD 2024* and the Grand Prix of the Open Call Competition at *Kobe Rokko Meets Art 2024 beyond*.  
https://masayamizuta.com
  
#### Akira Fujioka  
Born in Nagano Prefecture in 1997, Akira Fujioka completed his master's degree at the Graduate School of Advanced Science and Engineering, Waseda University, in 2022. He has been a member of metaPhorest since 2020, creating artworks and conducting research centered on silkworms. His practice seeks to reconsider the relationships between silkworms and humans by investigating the ecological characteristics of silkworms, as well as the cultural history of their relationships with humans, including customs, religion, and art.
